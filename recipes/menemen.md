# Menemen

Adapted from "Menemen" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Turkish eggs in spiced tomato — ripe tomatoes and sivri peppers sautéed until jammy and concentrated, then eggs poured directly in and folded gently until barely set, finished with a knob of butter, Aleppo pepper, and flat-leaf parsley. Breakfast, lunch, or late night — eaten from the pan with white bread.

Serves about 1 · 20 minutes · Vegetarian · Contains: egg, milk

## Ingredients

- 4 eggs, large
- 3 ripe tomatoes, cored and roughly chopped (400 g)
- ¾ green sivri peppers or Italian frying pepper, sliced (150 g)
- 1½ tablespoons unsalted butter (20 g)
- 1½ tablespoons olive oil (20 ml)
- Half a white onion, finely diced; optional — contested (80 g)
- 2½ teaspoons Aleppo pepper, or mild red pepper flakes, pul biber
- ¾ teaspoon fine sea salt
- ¾ teaspoon black pepper, freshly cracked
- A handful of flat-leaf parsley, roughly chopped

## Method

1. Heat the butter and olive oil together in a wide, shallow pan — ideally a sahan (copper pan) or small cast-iron skillet, 22–24cm — over medium heat. If using onion, add it first and cook for 3–4 minutes until translucent. Add the sliced sivri peppers and cook, stirring occasionally, for 5–7 minutes until the peppers are completely soft, limp, and faintly blistered at the edges. The peppers should be almost sweet by this point — green pepper's bitterness cooks away with time. Season with a pinch of salt.
2. Add the roughly chopped tomatoes to the peppers, along with half the Aleppo pepper, the salt, and the black pepper. Stir to combine. Increase the heat to medium-high and cook, stirring occasionally and breaking up the tomato pieces with a wooden spoon, for 8–12 minutes until the tomatoes have completely collapsed and the mixture is thick, jammy, and reduced — the water has evaporated and the mixture is clinging to the pan rather than flowing freely. The sauce should be deeply coloured and fragrant.
3. Beat the eggs lightly in a bowl — just enough to break the yolks and roughly combine, leaving streaks of white visible (these streaks create a marbled effect in the finished dish). Pour the eggs directly over the tomato and pepper base. Using a silicone spatula, fold the eggs gently through the tomato base in slow, wide strokes — pull from the edges inward, letting the tomato base streak through the eggs. Alternatively, leave the eggs mostly whole for a different style: nest the eggs whole on top and cover with a lid for 3 minutes until the whites set but the yolks remain runny.
4. Remove the pan from the heat. Scatter the remaining Aleppo pepper generously over the top — it should look like a dusting of dark rust-red across the yellow-orange surface. Scatter the chopped parsley. Serve immediately in the pan — menemen is traditionally brought to the table in its cooking vessel with a stack of white bread alongside for tearing and scooping. Do not let it sit: the carry-over heat from the pan will continue cooking the eggs, and the difference between perfect and overcooked is measured in minutes.
