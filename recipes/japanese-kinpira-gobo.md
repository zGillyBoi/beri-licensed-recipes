# Kinpira Gobo (Braised Burdock and Carrot)

Adapted from "Kinpira Gobo (Braised Burdock and Carrot)" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Julienned burdock root and carrot sautéed in sesame oil and simmered in a sweet soy glaze until each strand is tender but still has the faintest, satisfying resistance — a Japanese comfort side that rewards patience with the knife.

Serves about 2 · 35 minutes · Vegan · Contains: gluten, sesame, soy, wheat

## Ingredients

- 200 g burdock root, scrubbed, julienned; gobo
- ¾ carrot, peeled, julienned (100 g)
- 1½ tablespoons soy sauce, tamari for gluten-free (25 ml)
- 1½ tablespoons mirin (25 ml)
- 1½ tablespoons sake, or dry sherry (20 ml)
- 1 tablespoon toasted sesame oil
- ½ teaspoon dried red chili flakes, togarashi, optional
- 2 teaspoons toasted sesame seeds

## Method

1. Scrub the burdock root with a stiff brush under cold water — do not peel, the skin carries earthiness and nutrition. Cut into 5cm matchsticks (julienne), approximately 3–4mm square. As you cut, immediately transfer to a bowl of cold water with a splash of rice vinegar or lemon juice to prevent oxidation. Burdock discolors dramatically and quickly when exposed to air — within minutes it will turn from cream to dark brown. Drain and pat dry just before cooking.
2. Heat the sesame oil in a wok or medium skillet over medium-high heat. Add the chili flakes and stir for 10 seconds until fragrant. Add the drained, dried burdock matchsticks and the julienned carrot. Stir-fry over high heat for 3–4 minutes, tossing regularly, until the vegetables are beginning to pick up color and the burdock has softened slightly at the surface but is still firm at the center. The sesame oil should smell nutty and toasty.
3. Add the sake first — it will sizzle and reduce in 30 seconds. Add the mirin and soy sauce. Reduce heat to medium. Toss the vegetables in the glaze, then cook, stirring regularly, for 4–5 minutes until the liquid has reduced to a thin, glossy glaze that coats every strand. The burdock should be tender but with a slight, distinctive resistance at the core — kinpira is never soft. Taste for seasoning — it should be sweet, salty, and faintly earthy.
4. Transfer to a small serving dish or bowls. Scatter toasted sesame seeds generously over the top. Serve at room temperature as a banchan alongside rice. Kinpira gobo keeps refrigerated for 3–4 days and actually improves overnight as the glaze penetrates deeper into the burdock.
