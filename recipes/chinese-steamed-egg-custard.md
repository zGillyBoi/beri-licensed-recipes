# Chinese Steamed Egg Custard

Adapted from "Chinese Steamed Egg Custard" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Zhēng dàn — a savory custard so silky it trembles at the touch of a spoon, its surface smooth as polished jade, topped with a few drops of soy and sesame oil that pool into dark, fragrant islands.

Serves about 1 · 25 minutes · Contains: egg, fish, gluten, sesame, soy, wheat

## Ingredients

- 3 large eggs
- 1 cup warm water or dashi, around 60°C (240 ml)
- 1½ teaspoons light soy sauce, for custard
- A pinch of fine salt
- 1 teaspoon light soy sauce, finishing drizzle
- ½ teaspoon toasted sesame oil, finishing drizzle
- ¼ spring onion, finely sliced on the bias

## Method

1. Beat the eggs gently with chopsticks using a side-to-side motion — the goal is full integration without incorporating air. Add the warm (not hot) water and soy sauce. The water must be warm, not boiling: hot liquid pre-cooks the proteins and cold water sets them unevenly. Beat gently to combine, then strain through a fine-mesh sieve into the steaming bowl or individual cups. The sieving is non-negotiable — it removes chalazae and bubbles that create pitting and air holes in the finished custard. Skim off any foam from the surface with a spoon and cover tightly with cling film to prevent condensation from dripping onto the surface.
2. Set up your steamer with boiling water but immediately reduce to the lowest possible simmer before adding the custard. The enemy of smooth custard is aggressive steam. Place the covered bowl in the steamer, then turn the heat down so only a whisper of steam circulates. Steam on low for 12–15 minutes. Do not open the lid more than once to check. The custard is done when it no longer sloshes as one liquid but jiggles as a unified, soft gel. If your stove cannot sustain low enough heat, crack the steamer lid open 1–2 cm to let excess steam escape — this keeps the temperature from climbing too high.
3. Remove the cling film carefully — the accumulated steam and condensation will roll off. Drizzle the finishing soy sauce and sesame oil across the surface in two or three passes — they will pool into dark, fragrant islands on the pale custard. Scatter the spring onion across the top. Serve immediately in the steaming bowl, scooping portions at the table with a spoon. The custard is best the moment it's made; it begins to weep liquid as it cools.
