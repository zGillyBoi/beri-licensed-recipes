# Harira

Adapted from "Harira" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Morocco's beloved tomato-lentil-chickpea soup eaten to break the Ramadan fast — lamb, lentils, and chickpeas simmered with cinnamon, ginger, and coriander, thickened with a flour-tomato paste called tedouira and brightened with preserved lemon.

Serves about 4 · 1 hour 30 minutes · Contains: celery, gluten, wheat

## Ingredients

- 500 g lamb shoulder, cut into 2cm cubes
- 1 yellow onion, finely diced (200 g)
- ¾ cup green or brown lentils, rinsed (150 g)
- 1¼ cups cooked chickpeas, or canned, drained (200 g)
- 3 ripe tomatoes, grated or canned crushed (400 g)
- 3 tablespoons olive oil (40 g)
- 1½ teaspoons ground cinnamon
- 1½ teaspoons ground ginger
- 1¼ teaspoons ground turmeric
- 1¼ teaspoons black pepper, freshly ground
- 2 handfuls of fresh coriander, chopped; stems and leaves separated (30 g)
- A handful of flat-leaf parsley, chopped (20 g)
- 2 tablespoons preserved lemon rind, white pith removed, minced (20 g)
- 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour, for tedouira (30 g)
- ½ cup cold water, for tedouira, whisked with flour (120 g)
- 1¾ teaspoons fine sea salt
- 6½ cups water or light lamb stock (1500 g)

## Method

1. Set a large, heavy pot (at least 5 liters) over medium-high heat and add the olive oil. When the oil shimmers, add the lamb cubes in a single layer — do not crowd or the meat will steam rather than sear. Brown on two or three sides, about 3 minutes per side, developing a deep mahogany crust. Remove the browned lamb to a plate. In the same pot, reduce heat to medium and add the diced onion with a pinch of salt. Cook, scraping up the fond, for 5–7 minutes until the onion is soft and translucent. Add the coriander stems (reserve the leaves), cinnamon, ginger, turmeric, and black pepper directly to the onion. Stir and fry the spices for 60–90 seconds until they smell intensely fragrant and have dried out any remaining onion moisture. Return the seared lamb to the pot and stir to coat.
2. Pour the grated or crushed tomatoes over the spiced lamb and stir to combine, scraping up any fond from the base of the pot. Add the rinsed lentils and pour in the water or lamb stock. Bring to a vigorous boil over high heat, then reduce to a steady, gentle simmer — lid slightly ajar. Skim any grey foam that rises in the first ten minutes; this is coagulated blood protein from the lamb and you want it off the surface for a clean broth. Simmer for 45–50 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils have completely softened and begun to dissolve into the broth and the lamb is fully tender and yielding. The soup should have reduced and thickened slightly from the lentil starch.
3. In a small bowl, whisk the all-purpose flour with the cold water until completely smooth and lump-free — this is the tedouira, harira's traditional thickener and one of the things that makes it distinctly Moroccan rather than a generic lentil soup. Cold water is essential; hot water begins cooking the starch and creates lumps. Whisk vigorously, starting from the center and working outward, and tip the bowl to check the corners where dry flour hides. Let it stand 2 minutes, then whisk again — any remaining lumps will show up. A clean, perfectly smooth milky-white slurry is what you're after.
4. Add the chickpeas to the simmering soup and stir to incorporate. While the soup is at a steady simmer (not boiling), pour in the tedouira in a slow, steady stream while stirring constantly with a long spoon in wide circles. Pour slowly and stir continuously — this is the motion that prevents lumps. The soup will thicken noticeably within 2–3 minutes as the flour starch cooks out. Keep it at a gentle simmer for 5 more minutes to cook the flour completely; raw flour will make the soup taste starchy rather than silky. In the final minute, stir in the minced preserved lemon rind, chopped coriander leaves, and flat-leaf parsley. Taste and adjust salt. Serve immediately in deep bowls — harira waits for no one.
