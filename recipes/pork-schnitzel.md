# Wiener Schnitzel

Adapted from "Wiener Schnitzel" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Pork loin pounded thin as a page, encased in three precise coats of flour, egg, and breadcrumb, then fried in a deep wave of clarified butter until the crust billows away from the meat in a golden, gossamer shell that crackles on the first cut.

Serves about 5 · 40 minutes · Contains: egg, gluten, milk, wheat

## Ingredients

- 4 pork loin cutlets, 180–200g each
- ⅔ cup all-purpose flour (100 g)
- 3 large eggs, lightly beaten with a fork; not whisked
- 2½ cups fine, dry white breadcrumbs, not panko (150 g)
- 1¼ cups clarified butter or lard (300 ml)
- 1 teaspoon fine sea salt
- ¾ teaspoon white pepper, freshly ground
- 2 wedges of lemon wedges, for serving

## Method

1. Place a cutlet between two sheets of plastic wrap or inside a zip-lock bag. Using a meat mallet or the bottom of a heavy pan, pound the meat to an even 3mm thickness with firm, outward strokes — starting from the center and sweeping toward the edges to stretch rather than tear the meat. A properly pounded schnitzel should be uniformly thin across its entire surface, with no thick center or torn holes. Season both sides with salt and white pepper, then let rest for 5 minutes at room temperature so the salt begins to draw moisture to the surface.
2. Set up three separate wide dishes: flour in the first, beaten eggs in the second, breadcrumbs in the third. Working one cutlet at a time: first dredge in flour, pressing to adhere and shaking off every grain of excess — you want a ghostly, light dusting, not a thick coat. Dip immediately into the egg, allowing the excess to drip off for 3 seconds. Lay in the breadcrumbs and, critically, do NOT press the crumbs into the meat — scatter them over the top, and shake the tray to set them. This loose, unpressed crumb is what allows the crust to soufflé away from the meat in the fat, creating the billowing effect.
3. Pour clarified butter into a wide pan to a depth of 8–10mm. Heat to 160–170°C — test with a breadcrumb: it should sizzle immediately and turn golden in 30 seconds. Slide in the breaded cutlet. Immediately begin spooning hot butter over the top of the cutlet in a continuous stream while gently moving the pan to keep the fat agitated. This constant basting and movement is how the crust inflates. Fry 2–3 minutes per side until deep golden-amber. Never crowd the pan — fry one cutlet at a time if necessary. Remove to a wire rack (never paper) to drain.
4. Transfer immediately to warmed plates — schnitzel waits for no one and softens within minutes. Place one lemon wedge alongside, not squeezed over. Serve with potato salad, parsley potatoes, or lingonberry jam. The lemon is squeezed at the table by the diner, and only directly over their portion. Schnitzel eaten 10 minutes after frying is a different, lesser dish — time it to the moment the plates hit the table.
