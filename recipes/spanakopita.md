# Spanakopita (Greek Spinach Pie)

Adapted from "Spanakopita (Greek Spinach Pie)" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Crisp, golden phyllo pastry layered with a filling of wilted spinach, crumbled feta, dill and egg — each shattering, paper-thin sheet giving way to the creamy, herby, salty filling within. The kind of pie that disappears the moment it reaches the table.

Serves about 5 · 1 hour 30 minutes · Vegetarian · Contains: egg, milk

## Ingredients

- 400 g phyllo dough, fresh or fully thawed
- 4 bunches of fresh spinach, or frozen 500g, fully thawed and squeezed (1000 g)
- 2¾ cups feta cheese, crumbled (400 g)
- 3 eggs, beaten
- 4 spring onions stalks, thinly sliced; scallions
- 2 handfuls of fresh dill, roughly chopped (30 g)
- ½ cup olive oil or melted unsalted butter, for brushing (120 ml)
- ¾ teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
- A pinch of freshly grated nutmeg

## Method

1. If using fresh spinach, wilt it in a dry pan or with a splash of water over high heat until completely collapsed, then tip into a colander and cool. When cool enough to handle, squeeze the spinach in your fists — hard, relentlessly, handful by handful — until almost no water comes out. This is the most critical step in the recipe: residual spinach water makes the filling wet, turns the phyllo soggy, and ruins the finished pie. A kilogram of raw spinach will reduce to about 200g of dry, dark-green paste. Transfer to a large bowl and coarsely chop. Add the crumbled feta, beaten eggs, spring onions, dill, black pepper and nutmeg. Mix together with a fork until cohesive — the filling should look like a rough, green-flecked, salty mixture, not a smooth paste. Taste (carefully — the feta is already salty) and add more pepper or dill as needed. Set aside.
2. Preheat the oven to 190°C / 375°F. Brush a 30×22cm (or equivalent) baking pan generously with oil or melted butter. Work with phyllo quickly — it dries and tears within minutes of exposure to air; keep the stack covered with a damp towel between sheets. Lay the first sheet of phyllo in the pan, letting the excess hang over the edges. Brush the entire surface generously with oil or butter, reaching all the way to the edges and corners. Lay another sheet perpendicular to the first, brush again. Repeat this layering and brushing process until you have used roughly half the phyllo sheets (8–10 sheets depending on thickness). The fat between each layer is what creates the shatter-crisp, separated layers of the finished crust — do not skimp.
3. Spread the filling evenly across the phyllo base using a spatula, pressing it into an even layer and tucking it into the corners. Fold the overhanging phyllo edges inward over the filling — they will form part of the top crust. Now layer the remaining phyllo sheets over the top in the same oiled fashion, sheet by sheet, each one brushed generously. Fold or tuck the overhanging top sheets under the pie to form a neat border. Brush the entire top surface very generously with oil. Score the top layers only with a sharp knife in the portion sizes you want before baking — the classic diamond or square patterns — cutting through only the top 4–5 sheets, not all the way through to the filling. This prevents the crust from shattering uncontrollably when you cut after baking.
4. Bake at 190°C / 375°F for 45–55 minutes until the top is a deep, even golden-brown across its entire surface. Rotate the pan once at the halfway mark to ensure even browning. The pie is done when the scored lines have opened up and the phyllo between them has puffed and crisped, the edges are a slightly darker amber, and the kitchen smells of buttered pastry and herbs. Rest for at least 10 minutes before cutting along the pre-scored lines — the filling needs a moment to firm from liquid-eggy to sliceable. Serve warm or at room temperature; it is equally magnificent both ways.
