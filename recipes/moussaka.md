# Moussaka

Adapted from "Moussaka" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Layers of fried eggplant, spiced lamb and tomato sauce crowned with a thick béchamel and baked until the top sets to a trembling, golden custard — Greece's great baked dish, perfumed with cinnamon and sweet allspice, built for the afternoon and better the next morning.

Serves about 3 · 2 hours · Contains: egg, gluten, milk, sulphites, wheat

## Ingredients

- 2 large eggplants, sliced 1cm thick rounds (700 g)
- 2¼ cups lamb mince (500 g)
- 1 brown onion, finely diced (150 g)
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- A tin of canned whole peeled tomatoes, crushed (400 g)
- ½ cup dry red wine (100 ml)
- 1½ teaspoons ground cinnamon
- ¾ teaspoon ground allspice
- 2 bay leaves
- ⅓ cup olive oil, for frying eggplant (80 ml)
- ¼ cup unsalted butter, for béchamel (60 g)
- ⅓ cup plain flour, for béchamel (60 g)
- 2½ cups full-fat milk, warmed; for béchamel (600 ml)
- 2 eggs, beaten; for béchamel
- A pinch of freshly grated nutmeg
- ¾ cup kefalotyri or Parmigiano Reggiano, grated (80 g)
- 1¾ teaspoons fine sea salt and black pepper

## Method

1. Salt the eggplant slices liberally on both sides and let them sit in a colander for 20 minutes to draw out moisture — the collected liquid will be visibly briny. Pat every slice completely dry with paper towels. Heat a generous pour of olive oil in a wide heavy skillet over medium-high heat until it shimmers. Fry the eggplant in batches without crowding, 2–3 minutes per side, until deep golden-brown on both faces. The oil will be absorbed rapidly at first and then released again as the flesh collapses — this is the moment to flip. Drain on a paper-towel-lined tray. The slices must be properly golden, not pale: their caramelized surface is what holds the layers of the moussaka together.
2. In the same pan with a thin film of fresh oil, cook the onion over medium heat until softened and translucent, 8 minutes. Add garlic and cook one minute more. Add the lamb mince and cook, breaking it up with a wooden spoon, until no pink remains and the meat has taken on a light brown crust in places. Pour in the wine and scrape up any stuck bits — it will hiss and reduce to almost nothing in 2 minutes. Add the crushed tomatoes, cinnamon, allspice and bay leaves. Season generously with salt and pepper. Simmer uncovered on medium-low for 25–30 minutes until the sauce is thick, glossy and almost dry — moisture is the enemy of a clean-slicing moussaka. Remove bay leaves.
3. Melt the butter in a heavy saucepan over medium-low heat. Whisk in the flour and cook, stirring constantly, for 2 minutes until the roux smells of warm biscuit. Pour in the warm milk in a thin stream, whisking hard, adding it gradually to prevent lumps. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook 8–10 minutes, whisking often, until thick enough to coat the back of a spoon. Remove from the heat and let it cool for 5 minutes — you must cool it slightly or the eggs will scramble. Whisk in the beaten eggs, nutmeg and half the grated cheese vigorously. Season with salt and pepper. The result is richer, eggier and more custard-like than a plain béchamel — it will set in the oven like a savory flan.
4. Preheat the oven to 180°C / 350°F. Lightly oil a deep 30×22cm baking dish. Layer half the fried eggplant across the base in overlapping shingles. Spread the entire lamb sauce evenly over the eggplant, pressing it flat. Layer the remaining eggplant over the meat sauce in the same overlapping pattern. Pour the béchamel over the top, spreading it to the edges with a spatula — it should form an even layer at least 1.5cm thick, enough to set properly. Scatter the remaining grated cheese over the surface. Bake for 45–50 minutes until the top is a deep, even golden-brown and the edges are bubbling. Rest for at least 20 minutes before cutting — the internal structure needs time to set.
