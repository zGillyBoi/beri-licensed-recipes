# Yangzhou Fried Rice

Adapted from "Yangzhou Fried Rice" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

The most celebrated Chinese fried rice — day-old jasmine cooked over a blistering wok until each grain is separate and kissed with wok hei, bound with egg in two stages, studded with sweet shrimp, smoky char siu, peas and scallion. Prestige in a bowl.

Serves about 1 · 20 minutes · Contains: crustaceans, egg, gluten, molluscs, peanuts, sesame, soy, wheat

## Ingredients

- 3¼ cups day-old cooked jasmine rice, cold (600 g)
- 3 eggs
- 1 cup medium shrimp, peeled and deveined (150 g)
- 100 g char siu or honey-glazed ham, diced
- ½ cup frozen peas, thawed (80 g)
- 4 scallions stalks, thinly sliced
- 2 tablespoons light soy sauce (30 ml)
- 1 tablespoon oyster sauce
- 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
- ¾ teaspoon ground white pepper
- 3 tablespoons neutral oil, vegetable or peanut (45 ml)
- ½ teaspoon fine sea salt

## Method

1. This dish cooks in under 5 minutes once the wok is hot — everything must be ready before you touch the heat. Break up the cold rice with your hands, rubbing any clumps between your palms until every grain is separate and dry — clumped rice steams instead of frying. Beat the eggs lightly with a fork and a pinch of salt. Pat the shrimp dry with paper towels. Dice the char siu or ham into 1cm cubes. Have the sauces measured and combined in a small bowl. Slice the scallions with white and green parts kept separate. Line all components up beside the wok in the order they will be added.
2. Heat a wok or very large, heavy skillet over the highest possible heat for 2 full minutes — it should be smoking before any oil goes in. Add 2 tablespoons of the oil and immediately add the shrimp in a single layer. Sear without stirring for 45 seconds until pink and curled, then toss and cook 30 seconds more. Remove from the wok immediately. Add the diced char siu for 30 seconds just to warm and slightly caramelize the edges. Remove and set aside with the shrimp.
3. With the wok still blazing hot, add the remaining oil and swirl to coat. Pour the beaten eggs into the center of the wok — they will set almost instantly. Scramble them very briefly, just until they are 70% set but still wet and glossy. Immediately add the cold rice on top of the wet egg, pressing it down into the egg. Toss vigorously — every 30 seconds pause to let the rice sit against the hot wok, then toss again. This alternation between contact and toss is what creates wok hei, the smoky 'breath of the wok.' Cook for 3–4 minutes total.
4. Pour the combined soy sauce and oyster sauce over the rice and toss immediately and vigorously — the sauces evaporate quickly over high heat and should perfume rather than wet the rice. Add the peas and the white scallion parts, toss 30 seconds. Return the shrimp and char siu and toss just to incorporate and warm through. Finish with white pepper and a drizzle of sesame oil off the heat — sesame oil added to a hot wok becomes bitter. Scatter the green scallion tops over the top. Serve immediately — fried rice waits for no one.
