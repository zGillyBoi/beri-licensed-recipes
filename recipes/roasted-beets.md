# Salt-Crust Roasted Beets

Adapted from "Salt-Crust Roasted Beets" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Whole beets buried in coarse salt and roasted low and slow until the salt draws moisture and the beet's natural sugars concentrate into an intense, jewel-bright sweetness — a technique that takes far longer than foil-roasting but produces a flavor that is categorically different.

Serves about 6 · 2 hours · Vegan · Contains: sulphites

## Ingredients

- 4 whole beets, unpeeled, washed; medium-large (600 g)
- 5½ cups coarse sea salt or rock salt (800 g)
- 1½ tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil, for serving (20 ml)
- 2 teaspoons red wine vinegar or balsamic, for serving

## Method

1. Pour half the coarse salt into a deep baking dish to create a 2 cm bed. Nestle the whole, unpeeled beets on the salt, spacing them so they do not touch. Pour the remaining salt over and around the beets to cover them completely — they should be fully buried with no beet surface visible. The salt draws moisture out slowly and creates a sealed, even-temperature cooking environment, concentrating sugars far more than conventional roasting.
2. Roast in a preheated 190 C (375 F) oven for 1 hour 30 minutes to 2 hours. The salt will partially harden into a crust. To test for doneness, push a thin metal skewer through the salt into the largest beet — it should pass through with no resistance, like pushing into room-temperature butter. Small beets take 80 minutes; very large ones may need 2 hours 15 minutes.
3. Crack the salt crust with a spoon and remove the beets. The skin will peel off in large pieces using just your fingers and a paper towel (wear gloves if you don't want to stain your hands deep red for a day). Slice, quarter, or serve whole. Dress immediately with olive oil and a few drops of red wine vinegar or balsamic to add brightness to the concentrated sweetness.
