# Chawanmushi

Adapted from "Chawanmushi" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Silky Japanese steamed egg custard set with ichiban dashi — so delicate it barely holds its form, trembling when the cup is tilted, filled with shrimp and mitsuba.

Serves about 1 · 45 minutes · Contains: crustaceans, egg, fish, gluten, soy, wheat

## Ingredients

- 3 eggs, large, room temperature
- 1½ cups ichiban dashi, warm, not hot
- 1 teaspoon soy sauce, light, usukuchi preferred
- 1 teaspoon mirin, hon-mirin
- ¼ teaspoon fine sea salt
- 6 raw shrimps, peeled, deveined, tail on
- 4 slices of kamaboko, sliced; fish cake
- 6 mitsuba or trefoil sprigs, leaves; Japanese parsley

## Method

1. Beat the eggs gently with chopsticks or a fork in a cutting, slicing motion — do not whisk vigorously. The goal is to break the yolks and blend the whites and yolks into a uniform mixture without incorporating any air, which would create bubbles in the steamed custard. In a separate bowl or jug, combine the warm dashi, soy sauce, mirin, and salt and stir to blend. Pour the seasoned dashi into the beaten eggs gradually, stirring gently as you pour. The dashi-to-egg ratio of roughly 2.5:1 by volume is what produces the barely-set, trembling consistency — more egg and the custard becomes firm; less and it won't set. Strain the entire mixture through a fine-mesh sieve into a pouring jug, pressing the chalazae through with a spoon, then strain once more through cheesecloth if possible for an impeccably smooth result.
2. Divide the shrimp and kamaboko slices evenly among 4 chawanmushi cups (or any deep, heatproof cups or ramekins with lids). Arrange them with some care — in traditional service, the shrimp curl attractively against the cup wall where they can be seen through the custard once it sets, acting as a visual indicator of quality. If using shiitake, place them cap-side down. Leave space at the top — fill cups only to 80% capacity, as the custard needs room to set without touching the lid. Do not add the mitsuba yet; it will go on just before serving.
3. Pour the strained egg mixture slowly into each cup over the back of a spoon to prevent disturbing the fillings or creating bubbles. Fill to about 1 cm below the rim. Skim any bubbles off the surface with a paper towel or the edge of a spoon — any bubble on the surface will leave a crater when it pops during steaming. Cover each cup with its lid (or foil wrapped tight). Place in a bamboo steamer or a pot with a steaming rack. Steam over medium-low heat — not a rolling boil, just a gentle, steady steam. A violent boil will cause the custard to cook too fast at the edges, creating a pocked, spongy texture called 'su' (holes). Steam for 12–15 minutes. Check at 12 minutes: the custard should tremble as a single unit when gently shaken, with the center just barely set.
4. Remove the cups from the steamer and let them sit for 2 minutes — the custard will firm the final degree of setting from residual heat. Remove the lids and add 1–2 mitsuba sprigs to each cup, pressing the stems gently into the custard surface so they stand slightly upright. Serve immediately in the cup, with small spoons. Chawanmushi is eaten with a spoon by breaking through the smooth surface and scooping custard and filling together. It is served hot in winter and chilled (cold chawanmushi requires a firmer egg ratio) in summer. The mitsuba will wilt quickly — serve without delay.
