# Palak Paneer

Adapted from "Palak Paneer" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Golden paneer cubes nestled in a bright-green, velvety spinach purée spiced with garam masala and cream — the quintessential Punjabi vegetarian curry, vivid in color and deep in flavor.

Serves about 1 · 45 minutes · Vegetarian · Contains: milk

## Ingredients

- A bunch of fresh baby spinach, or full-leaf spinach, stems trimmed (300 g)
- 1⅓ cups paneer, cut into 2 cm cubes (200 g)
- 2 tablespoons ghee or neutral oil (30 g)
- ¾ yellow onion, finely diced (100 g)
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- A knob of fresh ginger, minced
- 2½ teaspoons garam masala
- 1½ teaspoons ground cumin
- 1½ teaspoons ground coriander
- ¾ teaspoon ground turmeric
- 1½ teaspoons Kashmiri chili powder or mild paprika
- 2½ tablespoons heavy cream (40 g)
- 1¼ teaspoons fine salt
- ¼ cup water (60 g)

## Method

1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a rolling boil. Drop in all the spinach and blanch for exactly 60–90 seconds — just until the leaves wilt completely and their color shifts from a dull grass-green to a vivid, electric emerald. Immediately drain and plunge into a bowl of ice water. The thermal shock locks both the chlorophyll and the bright color. Once cold, drain and squeeze firmly to remove as much liquid as possible. Transfer the squeezed spinach to a blender and blend with 2–3 tablespoons of water until you have a completely smooth, brilliant-green purée. Do not cook the spinach longer — every extra minute at heat turns it from jewel-green to army-drab.
2. Heat a non-stick or cast iron pan over medium-high heat. Add half the ghee. When it shimmers, add the paneer cubes in a single layer without crowding — work in batches if necessary. Sear undisturbed for 2 minutes until the bottom is deep golden. Turn and sear the remaining sides for another minute. The paneer will be resistant to cutting when raw; the seared surface gives it a slightly firm, savory shell that holds up in the curry rather than dissolving into it. Transfer to a bowl. Season lightly with a pinch of salt.
3. In the same pan, add the remaining ghee over medium heat. Add the diced onion and cook for 8–10 minutes until deeply golden and soft. Add the minced garlic and ginger and stir for 60 seconds until fragrant. Add all the ground spices — garam masala, cumin, coriander, turmeric, and Kashmiri chili — along with the salt. Stir constantly for 45–60 seconds, cooking the spices directly in the fat. This blooms the spice oils and removes the raw, powdery taste. The mixture will become very fragrant and dry; add a tablespoon of water if it begins to stick. The masala is ready when it smells intoxicatingly complex and the fat begins to separate visibly around the edges.
4. Pour the spinach purée into the masala and stir to combine. Add 2–3 tablespoons of water to help it incorporate. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for 5–7 minutes, stirring frequently. The spinach will darken slightly as it cooks into the masala but should retain a vivid green. Stir in the heavy cream and simmer for another 2 minutes. Taste and adjust salt. The gravy should be thick enough to coat the back of a spoon heavily — if it is very thick, add water by the tablespoon; if too thin, simmer uncovered for a few minutes longer.
5. Gently lower the seared paneer cubes into the palak gravy. Stir very gently — paneer is fragile and will crumble if handled roughly, so use a folding motion rather than stirring. Simmer together over very low heat for just 3–4 minutes, long enough for the paneer to warm through and absorb some of the curry's color and flavor at its edges. Do not cook longer — extended heat makes paneer rubbery. Serve immediately in a warmed bowl, with a swirl of cream on top if desired, alongside naan, roti, or basmati rice.
