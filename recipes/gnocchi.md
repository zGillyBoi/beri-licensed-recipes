# Potato Gnocchi

Adapted from "Potato Gnocchi" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Cloud-light pillow-dumplings made from still-warm baked potato, just enough flour, and a single egg — the Italian grandmother's paradox: the less you work the dough, the better the gnocchi. One extra gram of flour is the difference between a dumpling and a torpedo.

Serves about 3 · 1 hour 30 minutes · Vegetarian · Contains: egg, gluten, milk, wheat

## Ingredients

- 5 russet or Yukon Gold potatoes, floury variety (1000 g)
- 1⅓ cups all-purpose flour, plus extra for bench (200 g)
- 1 egg, beaten; large
- 1¼ teaspoons fine sea salt
- ⅓ cup parmigiano-Reggiano, grated; optional finish (30 g)
- 2½ tablespoons unsalted butter, for pan sauce (40 g)

## Method

1. Preheat oven to 200 C (400 F). Scrub potatoes, prick all over with a fork, and place directly on the oven rack (not wrapped in foil — you want moisture to escape). Bake for 55–70 minutes until completely tender through the center when pierced with a skewer and the skin is papery and tight. Do not boil the potatoes — boiling adds water, which demands more flour, which makes heavy gnocchi.
2. While potatoes are still steaming hot, halve them and scoop the flesh directly through a ricer or food mill onto a clean board or baking sheet. Spread the riced potato in a thin layer and let the steam escape for 3–4 minutes. Do not mash with a masher — it crushes the cells and creates a gluey paste. Cool just until you can handle it, but no more.
3. Make a well in the riced potato. Add the beaten egg and salt into the well. Scatter the flour over the top. Using a bench scraper or your fingertips, cut and fold the mixture together — do not knead. Work until just combined: a shaggy dough forms and no dry flour remains. The moment you see a cohesive mass, stop. Over-working develops gluten and makes gnocchi tough.
4. Flour your work surface lightly. Tear off a golf-ball portion of dough and roll it into a rope about 1.5 cm in diameter, applying even pressure from the center outward. Cut the rope into 2 cm pillows with a bench scraper. Optional: roll each piece over a fork or gnocchi board, pressing gently with your thumb to create ridges that hold sauce. Place on a lightly floured baking sheet in a single layer.
5. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a vigorous boil. Cook gnocchi in batches of 20–25 pieces. They are done the moment they bob to the surface and have stayed there for 30 seconds — no longer. Remove with a slotted spoon directly into a waiting skillet of browned butter over medium heat. Toss to coat and finish in the butter for 60 seconds until lightly golden.
