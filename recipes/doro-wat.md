# Doro Wat

Adapted from "Doro Wat" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Ethiopia's most celebrated national dish — chicken legs and hard-boiled eggs simmered in a dark, mahogany-red berbere and niter-kibbeh stew until the sauce reduces to a thick, impossibly fragrant paste that clings to every piece.

Serves about 7 · 2 hours · Contains: celery, egg, milk

## Ingredients

- 4 chicken legs, skin removed, scored; drumstick + thigh (1200 g)
- 6 hard-boiled eggs, peeled
- 4 yellow onions, very finely minced or grated (600 g)
- ⅓ cup niter kibbeh, Ethiopian spiced butter (80 g)
- ½ cup berbere spice blend (60 g)
- 1 tablespoon garlic cloves, minced to paste (20 g)
- A knob of fresh ginger, grated
- 1⅔ cups chicken stock or water (400 g)
- 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice (20 g)
- 1¾ teaspoons fine sea salt

## Method

1. This is the defining step of doro wat — and what makes it different from any other chicken stew. Place the finely minced onions in a large, heavy-bottomed pot with NO oil or butter. Cook over medium heat, stirring every 2–3 minutes, for 30–40 minutes. The onions will release their own water and slowly steam in it, then as the water evaporates they will begin to catch and caramelize. Stir more frequently as they darken. The goal is deep, mahogany-brown caramelized onion that has reduced to about one-fifth of its starting volume. Do not add water — the dryness is what develops the deep, complex sweetness that forms the backbone of the dish.
2. Add the niter kibbeh to the caramelized onions and stir until it melts into the onion mass. Add the berbere, garlic paste, and grated ginger. Stir vigorously to combine — the berbere will foam slightly as it hits the hot fat. Cook the berbere in the fat for 5–8 minutes over medium heat, stirring constantly. This step — blooming the spice in fat — is essential: it drives off the harsh, raw volatile acids in the berbere and deepens its color from a dull brick-red to a dark, glossy, almost black-red. The mixture should be very thick and fragrant, and the fat will begin to separate visibly at the edges.
3. Add the skinless, scored chicken pieces to the thick berbere-onion paste. Add the salt. Turn the chicken aggressively to coat every surface in the dark paste. Cook over medium heat for 5–7 minutes, turning the pieces frequently — you are not truly searing here (there is not enough dry heat), but you are coating the chicken with the paste and allowing it to begin cooking in the aromatic base. The paste will stick to the chicken as it begins to cook. Add the lemon juice and stir to deglaze any fond from the bottom of the pot.
4. Pour in the chicken stock or water and stir to combine — the liquid will immediately turn a deep brick-red and release the stuck paste from the pot bottom. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce to a low simmer. Cover and cook for 30 minutes. Then score the peeled hard-boiled eggs all over with a paring knife (deep slashes so the sauce can penetrate), add them to the pot, and continue simmering uncovered for 20–30 more minutes. The sauce should reduce significantly — the finished doro wat is very thick, coating the chicken and eggs in a dense, clingy mahogany sauce. The oil should separate visibly at the surface. If the sauce is still too thin after 30 minutes uncovered, increase heat slightly and reduce aggressively.
