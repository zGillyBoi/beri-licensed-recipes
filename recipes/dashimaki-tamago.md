# Dashimaki Tamago

Adapted from "Dashimaki Tamago" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

A Japanese rolled omelette scented with dashi, sweet with mirin, and built one thin golden layer at a time on a rectangular tamagoyaki pan — silky, spring-loaded, and trembling with custard softness within its spiral cross-section.

Serves about 1 · 25 minutes · Contains: egg, fish, gluten, soy, wheat

## Ingredients

- 4 large eggs
- ¼ cup ichiban dashi, cold (60 ml)
- 1 tablespoon mirin, hon-mirin
- 1½ teaspoons light soy sauce, usukuchi
- A pinch of fine salt
- 2 teaspoons neutral oil, sunflower or rice bran
- ¼ daikon radish, finely grated (50 g)

## Method

1. Combine the eggs, cold dashi, mirin, usukuchi soy sauce, and salt in a bowl. Beat with chopsticks using a side-to-side cutting motion — the goal is to break yolks and blend thoroughly without beating in air or creating foam. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve into a pourable container (a measuring jug works perfectly). The sieving removes chalazae and any undissolved white, guaranteeing a silky, lump-free result. The mixture should be a clear, golden amber.
2. Place the rectangular tamagoyaki pan over medium heat. Soak a folded paper towel in the neutral oil and rub it thoroughly over the entire inner surface — the towel goes back into the bowl for reuse between layers. Heat the pan until a drop of egg mixture sizzles immediately and sets within a second when dropped on the surface. The pan must be hot enough to cook each layer quickly but not so hot the eggs color or bubble before rolling.
3. Pour one-third of the egg mixture into the pan, tilting to coat the surface with a thin, even layer. As soon as the surface is about 80% set — no longer liquid but not fully dry — use chopsticks or a tamagoyaki spatula to roll the egg toward you from the far edge in 3–4 folds. Push the roll to the far end of the pan. Re-oil the empty space with the paper towel. Pour in the next third of egg mixture, lifting the existing roll briefly so egg slides beneath it. Repeat the roll toward you, incorporating the first log. Oil again, pour the last third, and roll once more. The finished log should have 3 distinct golden layers visible at the ends. Transfer immediately to a sudare (bamboo mat) or parchment paper and roll tightly into a cylinder. Press and hold for 1 minute to set the shape.
4. Unwrap the tamago and slice crosswise into 2 cm rounds with a sharp, wet knife. Arrange cut-side up to show the spiral. Place a small mound of freshly grated daikon beside each piece. The daikon is not merely garnish — its gentle enzymatic sharpness cleanses the palate between the sweet, custardy bites and balances the mirin's sweetness.
