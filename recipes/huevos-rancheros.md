# Huevos Rancheros

Adapted from "Huevos Rancheros" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Fried eggs settled onto a warm corn tortilla and smothered in a bracing roasted salsa roja — a Mexican ranch breakfast of pure contrasts: crunchy tortilla, runny yolk, smoky heat.

Serves about 2 · 30 minutes · Contains: egg, gluten, milk, wheat

## Ingredients

- 2 large eggs
- 2 corn tortillas, 15 cm / 6 inch
- 4 roma tomatoes
- 2 dried guajillo or ancho chilies, stemmed, seeded
- Half a medium white onion, halved
- 3 garlic cloves, unpeeled
- 3 tablespoons neutral oil or lard
- 1 teaspoon fine salt
- 2 tablespoons fresh cilantro leaves
- 2 tablespoons cotija cheese or queso fresco, crumbled
- 1 lime, cut into wedges

## Method

1. Heat a dry comal, cast-iron skillet, or flat griddle over high heat until very hot. Place the tomatoes, halved onion, and unpeeled garlic directly on the dry surface. Char them, turning occasionally, until the tomato skins blister and blacken in patches, the onion surfaces are dark brown, and the garlic is soft and fragrant — about 10 minutes total. Meanwhile, toast the dried chilies in the same dry pan for 30 seconds per side until they puff slightly and smell toasty but not burnt, then soak them in enough boiling water to cover for 10 minutes until pliable.
2. Peel the garlic. Add the charred tomatoes (with their juices), peeled garlic, browned onion pieces, and drained softened chilies to a blender. Blend until smooth — or leave slightly rough if you prefer a rustic texture. Season with salt. In a saucepan, heat one tablespoon of oil until shimmering, then pour the blended salsa in all at once — it will spit and splatter dramatically for 30 seconds. Fry the salsa, stirring, for 3–4 minutes until it darkens by a shade and the raw onion-garlic edge cooks out. The brief fry transforms blended ingredients into a cohesive sauce.
3. In a separate skillet, heat the remaining oil over medium-high heat. Pass each tortilla through the oil for 20–30 seconds per side — just long enough to warm through and pick up a few golden spots, but not enough to make them fully rigid. They should be pliable enough to fold but have a light crunch at the rim. Drain briefly on paper towels. Transfer to a warmed plate.
4. In the same skillet (or another), heat a film of oil over medium heat. Crack the eggs in carefully, keeping yolks intact. Fry sunny-side up: spoon hot oil over the whites to set the top edge, or cover with a lid for 1 minute to steam-set the whites without flipping. The whites should be fully set and opaque, with the yolk still completely liquid and bright orange-gold. Season with a pinch of salt.
5. Place the warm tortilla(s) on a plate. Slide the fried egg on top. Ladle the warm salsa generously over the egg white, leaving the yolk uncovered or just barely brushed so it remains visible and bright. Scatter crumbled cotija and fresh cilantro over the top. Serve with lime wedges on the side. Everything should arrive at the table hot — this is an assembly job that takes 30 seconds.
