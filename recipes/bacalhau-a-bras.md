# Bacalhau à Brás

Adapted from "Bacalhau à Brás" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Portugal's beloved salt cod scramble — thin-shredded bacalhau tumbled with shatteringly crisp straw potatoes and slow-scrambled eggs, then finished with black olives and vivid flat-leaf parsley. The dish is a study in contrast: salt and silk, crunch and cream.

Serves about 2 · 1 hour · Contains: egg, fish

## Ingredients

- 400 g salt cod, skin-on fillets, bacalhau
- 2 waxy potatoes, peeled (400 g)
- 6 eggs, large
- 1 white onion, finely sliced (200 g)
- 4 garlic cloves, sliced thin
- ⅓ cup extra-virgin olive oil (80 ml)
- A handful of flat-leaf parsley, roughly chopped (20 g)
- ½ cup pitted black olives, Kalamata or Gaeta (60 g)
- ¾ teaspoon fine sea salt
- ¾ teaspoon black pepper, freshly cracked

## Method

1. Place the salt cod in a large bowl of cold water, skin side up. Refrigerate and change the water three times over 24 hours — at 8-hour intervals is ideal. The fish should taste of the sea, not of brine. After soaking, pat thoroughly dry and remove any remaining bones with tweezers.
2. Lower the cod into a wide pan of barely simmering water (no rolling boil — the water should just tremble). Poach for 5–7 minutes until the flesh is cooked through but still moist. Remove with a slotted spoon and let cool enough to handle. Peel away the skin, then use your fingers and two forks to shred the cod into fine, thread-like pieces — as thin as you can manage without reducing it to mush.
3. Cut the peeled potatoes into matchstick julienne — about 2mm × 2mm × 6cm — using a mandoline or sharp knife. Rinse in cold water, then dry completely on towels (wet potato steams rather than fries). Heat 2–3cm of the olive oil in a wide pan to 180°C (a test straw should sizzle immediately). Fry the potatoes in batches until light gold and crisp, about 3–4 minutes per batch. Drain on a wire rack, not paper towels, which soften the bottom.
4. In the same pan (wipe out excess frying oil, leave a thin film), warm 2 tablespoons of fresh olive oil over medium heat. Add the onion and cook slowly for 10–12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until deeply soft, translucent, and faintly golden at the edges — this is the aromatic backbone of the dish. Add the garlic in the last 2 minutes and stir until fragrant but not coloured.
5. Add the shredded cod to the onions and garlic. Toss gently over medium-low heat for 1 minute to warm through and marry the flavours. Add the potato straws and toss again — you want them still crisp at this stage. Beat the eggs lightly with a fork (do not season — the cod does that) and pour over the mixture. Fold continuously with a spatula over low heat, pulling the eggs through the mixture in slow waves. Remove from the heat while the eggs are still a little underdone — they will carry-cook.
6. Scatter the roughly chopped parsley generously over the top — it is not just a garnish here but a bright, herbal counterpoint to the salt and richness. Add the olives, either scattered whole or halved. Drizzle with a thread of your best olive oil. Taste and adjust salt only if needed — it rarely will be. Serve immediately in the pan or on warm plates; this dish does not wait.
