# Baby Bok Choy with Garlic Oyster Sauce

Adapted from "Baby Bok Choy with Garlic Oyster Sauce" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.

Baby bok choy plunged into boiling salted water for ninety seconds emerges a shade of green so vivid it looks painted, still slightly firm at the stem and silky-tender at the leaf, arranged in a fan and blanketed with a glossy, golden garlic-and-oyster-sauce pan drizzle that smells of the sea and roasted allium. A dish that takes less than ten minutes from stove to table and asks nothing of you except timing and heat.

Serves about 2 · 10 minutes · Contains: celery, gluten, molluscs, sesame, soy, wheat

## Ingredients

- 4 baby bok choys, halved lengthwise (400 g)
- 2 tablespoons oyster sauce (30 ml)
- 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1½ tablespoons neutral oil (20 ml)
- 2 teaspoons light soy sauce
- 2 tablespoons hot water or light stock (30 ml)
- 1 teaspoon sesame oil, finishing

## Method

1. Bring a large pot of generously salted water to a vigorous rolling boil — it should taste as salty as light soup. The volume of water matters: too little water will drop dramatically in temperature when the bok choy is added, resulting in steaming rather than blanching and losing the vivid green color. The halved bok choy must go into aggressively boiling water. Lower each half carefully into the water cut-side down, using tongs. Blanch for exactly 75–90 seconds — the stems need the full duration, and the leaves cook in the residual heat. Lift out with tongs and immediately transfer to a plate, cut-side up, to prevent the flat cut surface from sitting in any pooled water. Do not run under cold water unless you are staging this dish more than 10 minutes in advance — the residual heat from within the stems will gently finish the leaves, and rinsing with cold water will wash away the natural sweetness from the cut surface. Arrange the halves on a serving plate in a fan or rows, all facing the same direction.
2. While the bok choy is being plated, add the neutral oil to a small saucepan or wok over medium heat. Add the sliced garlic and let it cook slowly, stirring occasionally, for 2–3 minutes. You are not trying to brown the garlic hard — the goal is fragrant, softened slices that are golden at the edges but pale gold overall, and that have released their pungent aroma into the oil. Fully browned garlic will taste bitter in the finished sauce. The oil will smell intensely of sweet, mellow roasted garlic within 2 minutes. Watch closely: thin slices go from pale to golden to burnt in under 60 seconds once browning begins. As soon as the majority of the slices are pale gold with golden-tan edges, remove from heat — the residual heat in the pan will continue cooking them for another 30 seconds.
3. Add the oyster sauce, light soy sauce, and hot water or stock to the pan with the garlic oil. The liquid will sizzle aggressively against the hot oil — stand back. Stir quickly to combine into a smooth, glossy sauce. Let it bubble for 15–20 seconds until the water has partially evaporated and the sauce has thickened slightly to a syrupy consistency. Remove from heat and add the sesame oil. Stir once. Pour the entire contents of the pan — oil, garlic slices, and sauce — directly over the plated bok choy. The sauce should pool around the base of the stems and coat the cut surface. The garlic slices will settle over the leaves. The heat of the sauce will warm the already-cooked bok choy without cooking it further. Serve immediately — this dish does not hold and must be eaten within 3 minutes of saucing, while the contrast between the crisp-silky bok choy and the warm glossy sauce is still live.
