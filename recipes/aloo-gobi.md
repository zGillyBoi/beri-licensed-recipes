# Aloo Gobi

Adapted from "Aloo Gobi" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

The homey Punjabi dry-sautéed curry of potato and cauliflower — both vegetables coated in turmeric and cumin and cooked until tender and speckled golden, with no sauce except the fragrant ghee they were tossed in.

Serves about 2 · 35 minutes · Vegetarian · Contains: milk

## Ingredients

- 1½ waxy potatoes, cut into 2 cm cubes; Yukon Gold (250 g)
- ¼ cauliflower, cut into small 2–3 cm florets (300 g)
- 2½ tablespoons ghee or neutral oil (35 g)
- 2 teaspoons cumin seeds
- ¾ yellow onion, thinly sliced (100 g)
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1½ teaspoons fresh ginger, finely grated
- 1¼ teaspoons ground turmeric
- 2 teaspoons ground coriander
- 1½ teaspoons garam masala
- 1¼ teaspoons Kashmiri chili powder or mild red chili powder
- 1¼ teaspoons fine salt
- A small handful of fresh cilantro, roughly chopped
- 1½ teaspoons lemon juice

## Method

1. Heat the ghee in a wide, heavy skillet or karahi over medium-high heat. Add the cumin seeds — they should sizzle immediately and begin to darken within 30 seconds. Add the sliced onion and cook, stirring frequently, for 8–10 minutes until deep golden and soft. Add the minced garlic and grated ginger and stir for another 60 seconds until the raw smell of the garlic has mellowed to sweet-savory. The entire pan should smell of toasted cumin and caramelizing onion — this is the flavor foundation everything else will absorb.
2. Add the ground turmeric, ground coriander, and Kashmiri chili powder to the golden onion in the pan. Stir constantly over medium heat for 45–60 seconds, cooking the spices directly in the ghee. The mixture will become very fragrant and stiff; if it begins to stick, add a tablespoon of water and stir to release. Season with salt. The spice paste is ready when the raw dusty aroma of the spices has transformed into a roasted, warm, complex fragrance and the fat has separated slightly at the edges of the paste.
3. Add the potato cubes to the spiced base and toss to coat every surface in the turmeric-orange spice paste. Spread into a single layer and cook over medium-high heat, stirring every 2–3 minutes rather than constantly — this allows the potato to develop golden sear marks rather than just steaming. Cook for 8–10 minutes until the potato cubes are golden on several sides and half-tender when pierced with the tip of a knife. They should still have a little resistance at this stage; they will finish cooking with the cauliflower.
4. Add the cauliflower florets to the pan and toss to coat in the spiced ghee. Sprinkle the garam masala over everything now — added at this mid-stage, it blooms gently in the residual fat rather than burning in an empty pan. If the pan looks very dry, add a tablespoon of water and cover with a lid for 3–4 minutes to steam-cook the cauliflower through. Then remove the lid and cook uncovered for another 5 minutes, tossing every 2 minutes, until both the potato and cauliflower are fully tender and the pan is dry — this is a dry curry; no sauce, only fragrant, spiced fat.
5. Remove the pan from the heat. Squeeze the lemon juice over the entire pan and toss immediately — the acid brightens all the spices and cuts through the richness of the ghee. Scatter the fresh cilantro over the top and give one final toss. Taste for salt and add a pinch more if needed. The finished aloo gobi should taste of nothing but the vegetables themselves and the spices — deeply savory, warmly spiced, with a clean lemony lift. Serve immediately with roti or paratha. It does not hold well; the vegetables lose their texture as they sit.
