# Arroz Rojo (Mexican Red Rice)

Adapted from "Arroz Rojo (Mexican Red Rice)" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Mexico's indispensable side dish — long-grain rice toasted in oil until each grain turns a pale blond, then simmered in a blended tomato, garlic and onion sauce until the grains are individually coral-red, fluffy and separate, perfumed with cumin.

Serves about 6 · 40 minutes

## Ingredients

- 1½ cups long-grain white rice (300 g)
- 3 plum tomatoes
- 2 garlic cloves
- ¼ white onion, quarter
- 2½ cups chicken or vegetable broth (600 ml)
- 1 serrano chile
- 1 carrot, peeled and diced small
- ½ cup frozen peas (60 g)
- 2 tablespoons vegetable oil (30 ml)
- ¾ teaspoon ground cumin
- 1¼ teaspoons fine sea salt

## Method

1. Combine the tomatoes, garlic, and onion quarter in a blender with 100ml of the broth and blitz to a completely smooth purée. You should have approximately 350ml of liquid. The raw purée will be bright orange-red; it will deepen during cooking. Set aside.
2. Heat the oil in a wide, heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat until shimmering. Add the dry (unrinsed) rice all at once and stir constantly with a wooden spoon for 4–6 minutes. The grains will gradually turn from white to pale blond, then to a warm golden color. Do not walk away — toasting is what keeps Mexican rice grains separate rather than clumping. The room will fill with a nutty, popcorn-like aroma.
3. Add the carrot and serrano chile to the toasted rice and stir for 30 seconds. Pour in the tomato purée — it will hiss and spatter as it hits the hot oil — and stir immediately to coat all the grains. Add the remaining broth, cumin, and salt. Bring to a boil, stirring once, then reduce to the lowest possible heat, cover tightly, and cook undisturbed for 18–20 minutes.
4. Remove from heat. Scatter the frozen peas over the surface, re-cover, and let rest 5 minutes — the residual steam will thaw and warm the peas without cooking them to mush. Then use a fork to fluff gently from the edges inward, pulling the grains apart. Remove the serrano chile before serving if desired. Taste for salt.
