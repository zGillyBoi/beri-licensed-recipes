# Kerala Tamarind Prawn Curry

Adapted from "Kerala Tamarind Prawn Curry" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Kerala-style prawn curry where shrimp simmer in a tangy, deeply spiced tamarind-coconut gravy with curry leaves, mustard seeds, turmeric, and dried red chiles — the sour tamarind balanced by sweet coconut and pungent fenugreek.

Serves about 3 · 35 minutes · Contains: crustaceans, mustard

## Ingredients

- 3⅓ cups raw prawns, peeled and deveined (500 g)
- 2½ tablespoons tamarind paste (40 g)
- ¾ cup full-fat coconut milk (200 ml)
- 2 medium white onions, finely sliced
- 5 garlic cloves, minced
- A knob of fresh ginger, minced (20 g)
- 4 dried red chiles, Kashmiri or byadagi
- 15 fresh curry leaves
- 1 teaspoon black mustard seeds
- ½ teaspoon fenugreek seeds
- ½ teaspoon ground turmeric
- 1½ teaspoons ground coriander
- 1 teaspoon red chile powder, or cayenne
- 2 tablespoons coconut oil (30 ml)
- 1 teaspoon fine sea salt

## Method

1. Heat the coconut oil in a wide, heavy-bottomed pan over medium-high heat until it shimmers. Add the mustard seeds — they will pop and splutter within seconds, scattering against the lid, so have it ready. As soon as the popping slows, add the fenugreek seeds, dried red chiles, and curry leaves in that order. The curry leaves will erupt violently in the hot oil, releasing their aromatic oils in a crackling burst. Stir once and allow 20–30 seconds for the fenugreek to toast to a pale gold.
2. Add the finely sliced onions to the spiced oil and stir to coat. Cook over medium heat, stirring regularly, for 10–12 minutes until the onions have melted down from their raw volume by more than half and turned a deep, even golden-brown — not blond, not mahogany. Add the garlic and ginger and fry for 2 minutes more, stirring constantly, until the raw smell of garlic cooks out and the mixture becomes sticky and fragrant.
3. Add the ground turmeric, coriander, and red chile powder to the golden onion base and stir vigorously to combine. Cook the spice mixture in the oil and onion paste for 90 seconds to 2 minutes, stirring constantly. This step — called bhunao in Hindi — fries the ground spices in fat, toasting them and driving off their raw notes. The mixture will look dry and clumpy at first, then loosen slightly as the spice oils release. Add a tablespoon of water if the spices begin to scorch.
4. Add the tamarind paste and stir it into the masala — it will hiss and sizzle. Pour in the coconut milk and salt, then stir thoroughly to combine everything into a smooth, uniform gravy. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for 5–6 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the gravy has thickened slightly and the raw sour edge of the tamarind has mellowed into the coconut. Taste: the gravy should be sour, warm with spice, lightly sweet from the coconut, and well-salted.
5. Add the peeled prawns to the simmering gravy in a single layer, pressing them down to submerge. Scatter a few fresh curry leaves over the top. Cook for 4–5 minutes over medium heat, turning the prawns once at the halfway mark, until they are fully pink and just cooked through. Remove from heat immediately — the residual heat of the gravy will continue cooking them if left on the stove. Serve with Kerala red rice, appam, or roti.
