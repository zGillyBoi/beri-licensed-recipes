# Moqueca Baiana

Adapted from "Moqueca Baiana" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

A Bahian fish stew of intoxicating depth — white fish simmered in coconut milk and dendê palm oil with tomatoes, peppers, and cilantro, everything cooked in a heavy clay pot that holds the heat like a loving embrace.

Serves about 3 · 1 hour · Contains: fish

## Ingredients

- 4 firm white fish, 4 cm chunks fillets, e.g. sea bass or cod (600 g)
- 2 tablespoons lime juice, freshly squeezed (30 ml)
- 2½ tablespoons dendê oil, red palm (40 ml)
- 1 white onion, finely sliced (150 g)
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 plum tomatoes, roughly chopped (250 g)
- 1 red and green bell pepper, thinly sliced (200 g)
- A tin of full-fat coconut milk (400 ml)
- A handful of fresh cilantro, stems and leaves separated (20 g)
- 1¼ teaspoons fine sea salt

## Method

1. Toss fish chunks with lime juice, a pinch of salt, and minced garlic (half the garlic). Cover and refrigerate for 20-30 minutes. The acid will begin to set the exterior proteins, helping the fish hold together in the stew without falling apart.
2. Heat dendê oil in a heavy clay pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add onion and cook 5 minutes until soft and turning golden. Add remaining garlic and bell peppers and cook 3 more minutes. The dendê oil will turn the whole base a vivid orange — this is correct and beautiful.
3. Add tomatoes and cilantro stems to the sofrito. Cook 5 minutes until tomatoes begin to dissolve. Pour in coconut milk and stir. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat. Season well with salt and taste — the broth should be rich, sweet from coconut, and slightly acidic from the tomato.
4. Nestle the marinated fish gently into the simmering broth in a single layer. Cover and cook over medium-low heat for 8-12 minutes, depending on thickness. Do not stir — use the lid to baste. The fish is done when it flakes easily at the thickest point and is opaque throughout. Scatter cilantro leaves over the top and serve straight from the clay pot.
