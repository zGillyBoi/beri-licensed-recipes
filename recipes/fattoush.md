# Fattoush

Adapted from "Fattoush" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

A crisp, restless salad of day-old flatbread toasted to shattering, tossed with tomato, cucumber, radish, and herbs under a dressing that punches with sumac — the tart, fruity spice that dyes the whole bowl a dusty rose and makes it impossible to stop eating.

Serves about 4 · 30 minutes · Vegan · Contains: gluten, wheat

## Ingredients

- 2 day-old pita breads, 2 rounds
- 3 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil, for toasting pita
- 3 medium ripe tomatoes, cut in wedges
- 2 persian cucumbers, sliced thin
- 6 red radishes, sliced into thin rounds
- 4 spring onions stalks, sliced thin
- ½ cup flat-leaf parsley leaves
- ¼ cup fresh mint leaves
- 3 tablespoons fresh lemon juice
- 4 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil, dressing
- 2 teaspoons ground sumac
- 1 teaspoon fine sea salt
- ½ teaspoon Aleppo pepper flakes, or chili flakes

## Method

1. Tear or cut the pita rounds into rough pieces — about 3-4 cm across, irregular shapes welcome. The ragged edges become the crispest parts. Place them in a bowl, drizzle with olive oil and toss so every piece is lightly coated. Spread on a baking sheet in a single layer and bake at 200 C (400 F) for 8-12 minutes, turning once halfway, until deep golden brown and completely rigid. Every piece must be fully toasted — any that remain soft will turn to mush the moment the dressing lands on them. Alternatively, fry them in a shallow pool of oil in a skillet over medium-high heat, turning frequently, for a richer, more golden result. Either way: they should shatter, not bend, when done. Let them cool completely before adding to the salad — hot chips steam the vegetables.
2. In a small bowl or jar, combine the lemon juice, olive oil, sumac, salt, and Aleppo pepper. Whisk vigorously or shake the jar until combined — it will not fully emulsify since there is no binder, but it should come together into a loose, tangy slurry. Taste it: it should be aggressively sour (from both lemon and sumac), fruity (sumac has an almost cranberry quality), and pleasantly spicy from the Aleppo. Sumac is not just decorative — it is the primary acidic agent alongside lemon and it stains everything it touches a dusty rose-pink. Let the dressing sit for 5 minutes so the sumac can bloom and soften slightly in the acid.
3. In a large wide bowl, combine the tomatoes, cucumber, radishes, spring onions, parsley, and mint. Season the vegetables with a pinch of salt directly and toss — this draws out a small amount of juice that joins the dressing. Add the sumac dressing and toss thoroughly, making sure every vegetable is coated. Finally, add the toasted pita chips and toss one last time — gently, so the chips don't shatter into fine crumbs. The chips must go in immediately before serving, never before. Fattoush waits for no one: once the dressing meets the chips, you have about 10 minutes before they lose their crunch. Serve in the widest bowl you own and eat immediately.
