# Korean Fried Chicken

Adapted from "Korean Fried Chicken" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

The double-fry is not a technique — it is a philosophy. First fry sets the crust and begins rendering the fat; second fry shatters the crust into something approaching glass, a shell so thin and crisp it crackles audibly across a room. The yangnyeom sauce — a lacquer of gochujang, honey, and garlic — clings without softening it, and the whole thing glows under kitchen light like lacquerware.

Serves about 4 · 2 hours · Contains: gluten, sesame, soy, wheat

## Ingredients

- 10 chicken wings or drumettes, or bone-in pieces (2 lb)
- ½ cup potato starch
- ¼ cup all-purpose flour
- ½ cup cold water
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt
- ½ teaspoon black pepper
- ½ teaspoon garlic powder
- 4 cups neutral oil for deep frying, canola or rice bran
- 3 tablespoons gochujang, Korean fermented red chile paste
- 2 tablespoons honey
- 1 tablespoon brown sugar
- 4 garlic cloves, minced to a paste
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
- 1 teaspoon rice vinegar
- 1 tablespoon toasted sesame seeds and sliced scallions

## Method

1. Pat chicken completely dry with paper towels — this is non-negotiable. Season with salt, pepper, and garlic powder. Toss to coat evenly. Let stand at room temperature for 30 minutes, or refrigerate uncovered for up to 2 hours for a drier surface and crispier result. The drier the surface, the crispier the crust.
2. Whisk potato starch and flour together in a wide bowl. Add cold water and mix until a thin, lumpy batter forms — do not overmix. The batter should be thinner than pancake batter and slightly lumpy. Add the seasoned chicken pieces and toss to coat thoroughly. Some pieces will have a thin, irregular coat; others will have drips and ridges — this inconsistency creates the craggly, ultra-crispy surface. Let battered chicken rest 5 minutes.
3. Heat oil to 330 F (165 C) in a deep pot. Fry the battered chicken in uncrowded batches for 8-10 minutes until lightly golden and cooked through. Remove and drain on a wire rack — not paper towels. Rest 10 minutes. Increase oil temperature to 375 F (190 C). Fry the same pieces a second time for 3-4 minutes until they turn deep amber-gold and audibly crackle when lifted from the oil. Remove and drain immediately.
4. Combine gochujang, honey, brown sugar, garlic paste, soy sauce, sesame oil, and rice vinegar in a small saucepan. Warm over medium heat, stirring, until the sugar dissolves and the sauce barely simmers — about 2 minutes. Do not boil. Working quickly, add the double-fried chicken to a large bowl and pour the warm sauce over. Toss rapidly to coat every piece. Garnish with sesame seeds and scallions. Serve within 5 minutes — the crust stays crisp for about 10 minutes after saucing.
