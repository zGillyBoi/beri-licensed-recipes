# Lomo Saltado

Adapted from "Lomo Saltado" by ForkRecipe Kitchen, from the ForkRecipe dataset.
This adaptation is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Peru's definitive chifa stir-fry — strips of sirloin and tomato hit in a screaming-hot wok with soy sauce and vinegar, tossed with fried potatoes and red onion into a juicy, smoky, uniquely Peruvian fusion dish.

Serves about 3 · 40 minutes · Contains: gluten, soy, sulphites, wheat

## Ingredients

- 600 g beef sirloin or tenderloin, sliced 1cm thick against the grain
- 2 yellow potatoes, cut into thick fries; Yukon Gold or similar (400 g)
- 2 red onions, cut into thick wedges; 8 wedges per onion (250 g)
- 3 ripe tomatoes, cut into thick wedges (300 g)
- 2 tablespoons ají amarillo paste, or 1 fresh ají amarillo, seeded and sliced (30 g)
- 4 garlic cloves, minced (20 g)
- ¼ cup soy sauce (60 g)
- 2 tablespoons red wine vinegar (30 g)
- 1½ teaspoons ground cumin
- ½ cup vegetable oil, for frying and wok (120 g)
- A handful of fresh coriander, roughly chopped; cilantro (20 g)
- ¾ teaspoon fine sea salt and black pepper
- 1⅔ cups cooked white rice, to serve (300 g)

## Method

1. Heat about 4cm of vegetable oil in a large deep pot or wok to 175C / 350F. Test with a potato strip — it should sizzle immediately and vigorously. Fry the potato strips in batches, never crowding the oil, for 6–8 minutes per batch until cooked through and golden on the outside. Transfer to a wire rack set over paper towel and season immediately with salt while hot — the salt adheres to the hot potato surface. Do not cover; you want them to stay crisp. These potatoes will be added back to the wok at the end. This double-presence of fries and rice in a single dish is one of the things that makes lomo saltado uniquely Peruvian — the carb abundance is intentional and joyful.
2. While the potatoes fry, season the beef strips with ground cumin, salt, and black pepper. Toss to coat evenly. Let the beef sit at room temperature for 10 minutes — this isn't a long marinade but a short seasoning rest that lets the salt draw a small amount of moisture to the surface, which helps the meat sear faster and more evenly. Crucial point: the beef must be dry on the surface before it hits the wok. Pat it with paper towels if any moisture has pooled. Wet beef steams rather than sears, and the sear is everything in lomo saltado — it's what creates the signature smoky, charred edges that no amount of sauce can substitute.
3. Heat your wok or largest cast-iron skillet over the absolute highest heat your stove can produce for two full minutes. Add a thin film of vegetable oil and let it begin to smoke — this is the signal that you're at the right temperature. Add the beef strips in a single layer and leave them untouched for 60 seconds to develop a deep, dark sear. Toss once and sear for another 30 seconds. Remove the beef to a plate — you want it cooked to medium, not beyond. In the same screaming-hot wok (add a splash more oil if needed), add the red onion wedges and garlic. Toss constantly for 90 seconds — you want the onion to char slightly at the edges while staying crunchy at the core. Add the tomato wedges and ají amarillo paste, toss for 30 seconds, then return the beef. Pour the soy sauce and red wine vinegar directly into the center of the wok — they will spit, steam, and partially vaporize, creating the characteristic 'chifa smoke' of lomo saltado. Toss everything together vigorously for 60 seconds.
4. Add the fried potato strips to the wok and toss once with the stir-fried beef and vegetables — just once or twice; you want the potatoes to pick up the dark, glossy sauce without going soggy from excessive stirring. Scatter the fresh coriander over the top and toss again. Taste a piece of beef and a potato: the beef should be savory, slightly charred, warm-spiced from the cumin, with the sour brightness of vinegar; the potato should be crisp outside and carry a coating of the dark sauce. Serve immediately, piling directly alongside white rice. The potatoes should still have their crunch — lomo saltado waits for no one.
